## CHAPTER III.

The excellency of God's work.

TO every *thing there is* a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven:

2 A time to be <sup>a</sup>born, and a time to <sup>b</sup>die: a time to <sup>c</sup>plant, and a time to pluck <sup>d</sup>up *that which is* planted:

3 A time <sup>e</sup>to kill, and a time to heal: a time to break down, and a time to build up:

4 A time to <sup>f</sup>weep, and a time to <sup>g</sup>laugh: a time to mourn, and a time to dance:

5 A time <sup>h</sup>to cast away stones, and a time to <sup>i</sup>gather stones together: a time to embrace, and a time to <sup>k</sup>refrain from embracing.

6 A time to get, and a time to lose: a time to keep, and a time to cast away:

7 A time to rend, and a time to sew: a time to <sup>l</sup>keep silence, and a time to speak:

8 A time to love, and a time to <sup>m</sup>hate: a time of war, and a time of peace.

9 <sup>n</sup>What profit hath he that worketh in that wherein he laboureth?

10 I have <sup>o</sup>seen the travail which God hath given to the sons of men to be exercised in it.

11 <sup>p</sup>He hath made every *thing* beautiful in his time: also he hath set the world in their heart; so that no man can <sup>q</sup>find out the work that God maketh from the beginning to the end.

12 I know that *there is* no good <sup>r</sup>in them, but for *a man* to rejoice, and to do good in his life.

13 And also that every man should <sup>s</sup>eat and drink, and enjoy the good of all his labour, it *is* the gift of God.

14 I know that whatsoever God doeth, it shall be <sup>t</sup>for ever: <sup>u</sup>nothing can be put to it, nor any thing taken from it: and God doeth *it* that *men* should fear before him.

15 That which hath been is now; and that which <sup>x</sup>is to be hath already been: and God requireth that which is past.

16 And, moreover, I saw under the sun the place of judgment, *that* <sup>y</sup>wickedness *was* there; and the place of righteousness, *that* iniquity *was* there.

17 I said in my heart, God shall <sup>z</sup>judge the righteous and the wicked: for *there is* a time there for every purpose, and for every work.

18 I said in my heart concerning the estate of the sons of men, that God might manifest them, and that they might see that they themselves are <sup>a</sup>beasts.

19 For that which befalleth the sons of men befalleth beasts; even one thing befalleth them: as the one dieth so dieth the other; yea, they have all one breath; so that a man hath <sup>b</sup>no pre-eminence above a beast: for all *is* vanity.

20 All go unto <sup>c</sup>one place; all are of the dust, and all turn to dust again.

21 Who knoweth the spirit of man that goeth <sup>d</sup>upward, and the spirit of the beast that goeth downward to the earth?

22 Wherefore I perceive that *there is* nothing better, than that a man should <sup>e</sup>rejoice in his own works; for that *is* his <sup>f</sup>portion: for who shall bring him to see what shall be after him?

## CHAPTER IV.

Vanity is increased unto men by oppression.

SO I returned, and <sup>a</sup>considered all the oppressions that are done under the sun: and, behold, the tears of *such as were* oppressed, and they had <sup>b</sup>no comforter; and on the side of their oppressors *there was* power; but they had no comforter.

2 Wherefore I <sup>c</sup>praised the dead which are already dead, more than the living which are yet alive.

3 Yea, better *is he* than both they which hath <sup>d</sup>not yet been, who hath not seen the evil work that is done under the sun.

4 ¶ Again, I considered all travail, and every right work, that for this a man is <sup>e</sup>envied of his neighbour. This *is* also vanity and vexation of spirit.

5 The fool <sup>f</sup>foldeth his hands together, and eateth his own flesh.

6 <sup>g</sup>Better *is* a handful *with* quietness, than both the hands full *with* travail and vexation of spirit.

7 ¶ Then I returned, and I saw vanity under the sun.

8 There is one *alone* and *there is* not <sup>h</sup>a second; yea, he hath neither child nor brother: yet *is there* no end of all his labour; neither is his <sup>i</sup>eye satisfied with riches; neither *saith he,* For whom do I labour, and bereave my soul of good? This *is* also vanity, yea, it *is* a sore travail.

9 ¶ <sup>k</sup>Two *are* better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour.

10 For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but wo to him *that is* <sup>l</sup>alone when he falleth; for *he hath* not another to help him up.

11 Again, if <sup>m</sup>two lie together, then they have heat: but how can one be warm *alone?*

12 And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and <sup>n</sup>a threefold cord is not quickly broken.

13 ¶ Better *is* a <sup>o</sup>poor and a wise child than an old and foolish king, who will no more <sup>p</sup>be admonished.

14 For out of prison he cometh <sup>q</sup>to reign; whereas also *he that is* born in his kingdom becometh <sup>r</sup>poor.

15 I considered all the living which walk under the sun, with the <sup>s</sup>second child that shall stand up in his stead.

16 *There is* no <sup>t</sup>end of all the people, *even* of all that have been before them: they also that come after shall <sup>u</sup>not rejoice in him. Surely this also *is* vanity and vexation of spirit.

Vanities in divine service, and in murmuring against oppression.

2 Be not rash with thy mouth, and let not thy heart be hasty to utter *any* thing before God; for God *is* in heaven, and thou upon <sup>c</sup>earth: therefore let thy words be <sup>d</sup>few.

3 For a dream cometh through the multitude of business; and <sup>e</sup>a fool's voice *is known* by multitude of words.
