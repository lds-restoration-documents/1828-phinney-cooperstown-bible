## CHAPTER III.

Titus is yet further directed by Paul, both concerning the things he should teach, and not teach, &c.

<sup>a</sup>PUT them in mind to be subject to principalities and powers, to obey magistrates, to be ready to every good work,

2 <sup>b</sup>To speak evil of no man, to be no brawlers, *but* gentle, shewing all meekness unto all men.

3 <sup>c</sup>For we ourselves also were sometimes foolish, disobedient, deceived, serving divers lusts and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, *and* hating one another.

4 <sup>d</sup>But after that the kindness and love of God our Saviour toward man appeared,

5 <sup>e</sup>Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;

6 <sup>f</sup>Which he shed on us abundantly through Jesus Christ our Saviour;

7 <sup>g</sup>That, being justified by his grace, we should be made heirs according to the hope of eternal life.

8 <sup>h</sup>*This is* a faithful saying; and these things I will that thou affirm constantly; that they which have believed in God might be careful to <sup>†</sup>maintain good works. These things are good and profitable unto men.

9 <sup>i</sup>But avoid foolish questions, and genealogies, and contentions, and strivings about the law; for they are unprofitable and vain.

10 <sup>k</sup>A man that is a heretic, after the first and second admonition, reject;

11 Knowing that he that is such, is subverted, and sinneth, being condemned of himself.

12 <sup>l</sup>When I shall send Artemas unto thee, or Tychicus, be diligent to come unto me to Nicopolis: for I have determined there to winter.

13 <sup>m</sup>Bring Zenas the lawyer, and Apollos on their journey diligently, that nothing be wanting unto them.

14 <sup>n</sup>And let ours also learn to maintain good works for necessary uses, that they be not unfruitful.

15 All that are with me salute thee. Greet them that love us in the faith. Grace *be* with you all. Amen.

¶ It was written to Titus, ordained the first bishop of the church of the Cretians, from Nicopolis of Macedonia.

# ¶ The Epistle of PAUL to PHILEMON.

1 Paul's joy in hearing of the faith and love of Philemon. 10 Onesimus entreated for.

<sup>a</sup>PAUL, a prisoner of Jesus Christ, and Timothy *our* brother, unto Philemon our dearly beloved, and fellow-labourer,

2 <sup>b</sup>And to *our* beloved Apphia, and Archippus our fellow-soldier, and to the church in thy house:

3 Grace to you, and peace, from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

4 <sup>c</sup>I thank my God, making mention of thee always in my prayers,

5 <sup>d</sup>Hearing of thy love and faith, which thou hast toward the Lord Jesus, and toward all saints;

6 <sup>e</sup>That the communication of thy faith may become effectual by the acknowledging of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus.

7 <sup>f</sup>For we have great joy and consolation in thy love, because the bowels of the saints are refreshed by thee, brother.

8 <sup>g</sup>Wherefore, though I might be much bold in Christ to enjoin thee that which is convenient,

9 Yet for love's sake I rather beseech *thee,* being such a one as Paul the aged, and now also a prisoner of Jesus Christ.

10 <sup>h</sup>I beseech thee for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds:

11 Which in time past was to thee unprofitable, but now profitable to thee and to me:

12 Whom I have sent again: thou therefore receive him, that is mine own bowels:

13 <sup>i</sup>Whom I would have retained with me, that in thy stead he might have ministered unto me in the bonds of the gospel:

14 <sup>k</sup>But without thy mind would I do nothing; that thy benefit should not be as it were of necessity, but willingly.

15 <sup>l</sup>For perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever;

16 <sup>m</sup>Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved, especially to me, but how much more unto thee, both in the flesh and in the Lord?

17 If thou count me therefore a partner, receive him as myself.

18 If he hath wronged thee, or oweth *thee* aught, put that on mine account;

19 I Paul have written *it* with mine own hand, I will repay *it:* albeit I do not say to thee how thou owest unto me even thine own self besides.

20 Yea, brother, let me have joy of thee in the Lord: refresh my bowels in the Lord.

21 <sup>n</sup>Having confidence in thy obedience, I wrote unto thee, knowing that thou wilt also do more than I say.

22 <sup>o</sup>But withal prepare me also a lodging: for I trust that through your prayers I shall be given unto you.

23 <sup>p</sup>There salute thee Epaphras, my fellow-prisoner in Christ Jesus;

24 <sup>q</sup>Marcus, Aristarchus, Demas, Lucas, my fellow-labourers.

25 The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ *be* with your spirit. Amen.

¶ Written from Rome to Philemon, by Onesimus a servant.
