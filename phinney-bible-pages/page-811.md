## CHAPTER II.

1 The reasons of Paul's not coming unto them....6 Of the excommunicated person.

<sup>a</sup>BUT I determined this with myself, that I would not come again to you in heaviness.

2 <sup>b</sup>For if I make you sorry, who is he then that maketh me glad, but the same which is made sorry by me?

3 And I wrote this same unto you, lest, when I came, I should have sorrow from them of whom I ought to rejoice; having confidence in you all, that my joy is *the joy* of you all.

4 <sup>c</sup>For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote unto you with many tears; not that ye should be grieved, but that ye might know the love which I have more abundantly unto you.

5 <sup>d</sup>But if any have caused grief, he hath not grieved me, but in part: that I may not overcharge you all.

6 <sup>e</sup>Sufficient to such a man *is* this punishment, which *was inflicted* of many.

7 <sup>f</sup>So that contrariwise ye *ought* rather to forgive *him,* and comfort *him,* lest perhaps such a one should be swallowed up with overmuch sorrow.

8 Wherefore I beseech you, that ye would confirm *your* love toward him.

9 <sup>g</sup>For to this end also did I write, that I might know the proof of you, whether ye be obedient in all things.

10 <sup>h</sup>To whom ye forgive any thing, I *forgive* also: for if I forgave any thing, to whom I forgave *it,* for your sakes *forgave I it* in the person of Christ;

11 <sup>i</sup>Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices.

12 <sup>k</sup>Furthermore, when I came to Troas to preach <!-- uncertain: a stray mark/blot printed beside the p of "preach" --> Christ's gospel, and a door was opened unto me of the Lord,

13 <sup>l</sup>I had no rest in my spirit, because I found not Titus my brother: but taking my leave of them, I went from thence into Macedonia.

14 <sup>m</sup>Now thanks *be* unto God, which always causeth us to triumph in Christ, and maketh manifest the savour of his knowledge by us in every place.

15 <sup>n</sup>For we are unto God a sweet savour of Christ, in them that are saved, and in them that perish:

16 <sup>o</sup>To the one *we are* the savour of death unto death; and to the other the savour of life unto life. And who *is* sufficient for these things?

17 <sup>p</sup>For we are not as many, which corrupt the word of God: but as of sincerity, but as of God, in the sight of God, speak we in Christ.

## CHAPTER III.

He sheweth that their gifts and graces were a sufficient commendation of his ministry.

<sup>a</sup>DO we begin again to commend ourselves? or need we, as some *others,* epistles of commendation to you, or *letters* of commendation from you?

2 <sup>b</sup>Ye are our epistle written in our hearts, known and read of all men:

3 <sup>c</sup>*Forasmuch as ye are* manifestly declared to be the epistle of Christ ministered by us, written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God: not in tables of stone, but in fleshly tables of the heart.

4 <sup>d</sup>And such trust have we through Christ to God-ward:

5 <sup>e</sup>Not that we are sufficient of ourselves to think any thing as of ourselves; but our sufficiency *is* of God;

6 <sup>f</sup>Who also hath made us able ministers of the new testament; not of the letter, but of the spirit: for the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life.

7 <sup>g</sup>But if the ministration of death, written *and* engraven in stones, was glorious, so that the children of Israel could not steadfastly behold the face of Moses for the glory of his countenance; which *glory* was to be done away;

8 How shall not the ministration of the Spirit be rather glorious?

9 <sup>h</sup>For if the ministration of condemnation *be* glory, much more doth the ministration of righteousness exceed in glory.

10 For even that which was made glorious had no glory in this respect, by reason of the glory that excelleth.

11 For if that which is done away *was* glorious, much more that which remaineth *is* glorious.

12 <sup>i</sup>Seeing then that we have such hope, we use great plainness of speech:

13 <sup>k</sup>And not as Moses, *which* put a vail over his face, that the children of Israel could not steadfastly look to the end of that which is abolished:

14 <sup>l</sup>But their minds were blinded; for until this day remaineth the same vail untaken away in the reading of the old testament: which *vail* is done away in Christ.

15 But even unto this day, when Moses is read, the vail *is* upon their heart.

16 <sup>m</sup>Nevertheless, when it shall turn to the Lord, the vail shall be taken away.

17 <sup>n</sup>Now the Lord is that Spirit: and where the Spirit of the Lord *is,* there *is* liberty.

18 <sup>o</sup>But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, *even* as by the Spirit of the Lord.

## CHAPTER IV.

1 Paul's unwearied diligence and zeal in preaching; 7 his troubles for the same.

<sup>a</sup>THEREFORE, seeing we have this ministry, as we have received mercy, we faint not;

2 <sup>b</sup>But have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness, nor handling the word of God deceitfully; but by manifestation of the truth, commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God.

3 <sup>c</sup>But if our gospel be hid, it is hid to them that are lost:

4 <sup>d</sup>In whom the god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not, lest the light of the glorious gospel of Christ, who is the image of God, should shine unto them.

5 <sup>e</sup>For we preach not ourselves, but Christ Jesus the Lord; and ourselves your servants for Jesus' sake.

6 <sup>f</sup>For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to *give* the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.

7 <sup>g</sup>But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us.

8 <sup>h</sup>*We are* troubled on every side, yet not distressed; *we are* perplexed, but not in despair;

9 <sup>i</sup>Persecuted, but not forsaken; cast down, but not destroyed;
