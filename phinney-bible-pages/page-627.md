16 That they might know, that <sup>k</sup>wherewithal a man sinneth, by the same also shall he be punished.

17 For thine Almighty hand, that made the world of matter <sup>l</sup>without form, wanted not means to send among them a multitude of bears, or fierce lions,

18 Or unknown <sup>m</sup>wild beasts, full of rage, newly created, breathing out either a fiery vapour, or filthy scents of scattered smoke, or shooting horrible sparkles out of their eyes:

19 Whereof not only the harm might despatch them at once, but also the terrible sight utterly destroy them.

20 Yea, and without these might they have fallen down with one blast, being persecuted of vengeance, and scattered abroad through the breath of thy power: but thou hast ordered all things in measure and number and weight.

21 For thou canst shew thy great strength at all times when thou wilt; and who may withstand <sup>n</sup>the power of thine arm?

22 For the whole world before thee is as a <sup>||</sup>little grain of the balance, yea, as a drop of the morning dew that falleth down upon the earth.

23 But thou hast mercy upon all; for thou canst do all things, and <sup>o</sup>winkest at the sins of men, because they should amend.

24 For thou lovest all the things that are, <sup>p</sup>and abhorrest nothing which thou hast made: for never wouldest thou have made any thing, if thou hadst hated it.

25 And how could any thing have endured, if it had not been thy will? or been preserved, if not called by thee?

26 But <sup>q</sup>thou sparest all: for they are thine, O Lord, thou lover of souls.

## CHAPTER XII.

2 God did not destroy those of Chanaan all at once.....12 If he had done so, who could control him?.....19 But by sparing them, he taught us.....27 They were punished with their gods.

FOR thine incorruptible <sup>a</sup>Spirit is in all things.

2 Therefore <sup>b</sup>chasteneth thou them by little and little that offend, and warnest them by putting them in remembrance wherein they have offended, that leaving their wickedness they may believe on thee, O Lord.

3 For it was thy will to destroy by the hands of our fathers both those <sup>||</sup>old inhabitants of thy holy land,

4 Whom thou hatedst for doing most odious works of <sup>||</sup>witchcrafts, and wicked sacrifices;

5 And also those <sup>c</sup>merciless murderers of children, and devourers of man's flesh, and the feasts of blood,

6 With their priests out of the midst of their idolatrous crew, and the parents that killed with their own hands souls destitute of help:

7 That the land, which thou esteemedst above all other, might receive a worthy <sup>||</sup>colony of God's children.

8 Nevertheless, even those thou sparedst as men, and didst send <sup>d</sup>wasps, forerunners of thy host, to destroy them by little and little.

9 <sup>e</sup>Not that thou wast unable to bring the ungodly under the hand of the righteous in battle, or to destroy them at once with cruel beasts, or with one rough word:

10 But executing thy judgments upon them by little, and little, <sup>f</sup>thou gavest them place of repentance, not being ignorant that they were a naughty generation, and that their malice was bred in them, and that their cogitation would never be changed.

11 For it was a <sup>g</sup>cursed seed from the beginning; neither didst thou for fear of any man give them pardon for those things wherein they sinned.

12 For who shall say, <sup>h</sup>What hast thou done? or who shall withstand thy judgment? or who shall accuse thee for the nations that perish, whom thou hast made? or who shall come to stand <sup>||</sup>against thee, to be <sup>||</sup>revenged for the unrighteous men?

13 For neither is there any God but thou that <sup>i</sup>careth for all, to whom thou mightest shew that thy judgment is not unright.

14 <sup>k</sup>Neither shall king or tyrant be able to set his face against thee for any of whom thou hast punished.

15 Forsomuch then as thou art righteous thyself, thou orderest all things righteously: <sup>l</sup>thinking it not agreeable with thy power to condemn him that hath not deserved to be punished.

16 For thy power is the beginning of righteousness, and because thou art the Lord of all, <sup>m</sup>it maketh thee to be gracious unto all.

17 For when men will not believe that thou art of a <sup>||</sup>full power, thou shewest thy strength, and among them that know it thou makest their boldness manifest.

18 But thou mastering thy power, judgest with equity, and orderest us with great favour: <sup>n</sup>for thou mayest use power when thou wilt.

19 But by such works hast thou taught thy people that the just man should be <sup>o</sup>merciful, and hast made thy children to be of a good hope that thou givest repentance for sins.

20 For if thou didst punish the enemies of thy children, and the condemned to death, with such deliberation, giving them time and place, whereby they might be delivered from their malice:

21 With how great circumspection didst thou judge thine own sons, <sup>p</sup>unto whose fathers thou hast sworn, and made covenants of good promises?

22 Therefore, whereas thou dost chasten us, thou scourgest our enemies a thousand times more, to the intent that, when we judge we should carefully think of thy goodness, and when we ourselves are judged, we should look for mercy. <!-- the margin letter q (Prov. 3, 11, 12) has no visible matching mark in the text -->

23 Wherefore, whereas men have lived dissolutely and unrighteously, thou hast tormented them with their own <sup>||</sup>abominations.

24 <sup>r</sup>For they went astray very far in the ways of error, and held them for gods, which even among the beasts of their enemies were despised, being deceived, as children of no understanding.

25 Therefore unto them, as to children without the use of reason, thou didst send a judgment to <sup>s</sup>mock them.

26 But they that would not be reformed by that correction, wherein he dallied with them, shall feel a judgment worthy of God.

27 For, look, for what things they grudged, when they were punished, that is, for them whom they thought to be gods; [now] being punished in them, when they saw it, they acknowledged him to be the true God, whom before they denied <sup>u</sup>to know; and therefore came extreme damnation upon them.

## CHAPTER XIII.

1 They were not excused that worshipped any of God's works.....10 But most wretched are they that worship the works of men's hands.

SURELY vain are all men by nature, who are ignorant of God, and <sup>a</sup>could not out of the good things that are seen know him that is: neither, by considering the works, did they acknowledge the work-master;

2 <sup>b</sup>But deemed either fire, or wind, or the swift air, or the circle of the stars, or the violent water, or <sup>c</sup>the lights of heaven, to be the gods which govern the world.

3 With whose beauty if they <sup>d</sup>being delighted took them to be gods; let them know how much better the Lord of them is: for the first author of beauty hath created them.

4 But if they were astonished at their power and virtue, let them understand by them, how much mightier he is that made them.

5 For by the greatness and beauty of the creatures, proportionably the maker of them <sup>e</sup>is seen.

6 But yet for this they are the less to be blamed: for they peradventure err, seeking God, and desirous to find him.

7 For being <sup>f</sup>conversant in his works, they <sup>||</sup>search *him* diligently, and believe their sight: because the things are beautiful that are seen.

8 Howbeit, <sup>g</sup>neither are they to be pardoned.

9 For if they were able to know so much, that they could aim at the world; how did they not sooner find out the Lord thereof?

10 But miserable are they, and in <sup>h</sup>dead things is their hope, who called them gods <sup>i</sup>which are the works of men's hands, gold and silver, to shew art in, and resemblances of beasts, or a stone good for nothing, the work of an ancient hand.

11 <sup>k</sup>Now a <sup>||</sup>carpenter that felleth timber, after he hath sawn down a tree meet for the purpose, and taken off all the bark skilfully round about, and hath wrought it handsomely, and made a vessel thereof fit for the service of man's life;

12 And after spending the <sup>||</sup>refuse of his work to dress his meat, hath filled himself;

13 And taking the very refuse among those which served to no use, being a crooked piece of wood, and full of knots, hath carved it diligently, when he had nothing else to do, <sup>l</sup>and formed it by the skill of his understanding, and fashioned it to the image of a man;
