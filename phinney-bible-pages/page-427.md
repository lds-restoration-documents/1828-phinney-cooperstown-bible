## CHAPTER X.

Observations of wisdom, folly, riot, and slothfulness.

DEAD flies cause the ointment of the apothecary to send forth a stinking savour: *so doth* <sup>a</sup>a little folly him that is in reputation for wisdom *and* honour.

2 A wise man's heart *is* at his <sup>b</sup>right hand; but a fool's heart at <sup>c</sup>his left.

3 Yea also, when he that is a fool walketh by the way, his wisdom faileth *him,* and <sup>d</sup>he saith to every one *that* he *is* a fool.

4 If the spirit of the ruler rise up against thee, leave not thy place; for <sup>e</sup>yielding pacifieth great offences.

5 There is an evil *which* I have seen under the sun, as an <sup>f</sup>error *which* proceedeth from the ruler;

6 Folly is set in great dignity, and the <sup>g</sup>rich sit in low place.

7 I have seen <sup>h</sup>servants upon horses, and princes walking as servants upon the earth.

8 He that <sup>i</sup>diggeth a pit shall fall into it; and whoso <sup>k</sup>breaketh a hedge, a serpent shall bite him.

9 Whoso removeth stones shall be hurt therewith; *and* he that cleaveth wood shall be endangered thereby.

10 If the iron be blunt, and he do not whet the edge, then must he put to more strength: but <sup>l</sup>wisdom *is* profitable to direct.

11 Surely the <sup>m</sup>serpent will bite without enchantment; and a babbler is no better.

12 The words of a wise man's mouth *are* <sup>n</sup>gracious: but the lips of a fool will <sup>o</sup>swallow up himself.

13 The <sup>p</sup>beginning of the words of his mouth *is* foolishness: and the end of his talk *is* mischievous madness.

14 A fool also is <sup>q</sup>full of words: a man cannot tell what shall be; and what shall be after him who can tell him?

15 The labour of the foolish wearieth every one of them; because he knoweth not how to go to the <sup>r</sup>city.

16 Wo to thee, O land, when thy king *is* <sup>s</sup>a child, and thy princes eat in the morning!

17 Blessed *art* thou, O land, when thy king *is* the son of <sup>t</sup>nobles, and thy princes eat in due season, for strength, and not for drunkenness!

18 By much slothfulness the building decayeth; and through idleness of the hands the house droppeth through.

19 A feast is made for laughter, and wine maketh merry: but money answereth all *things.*

20 <sup>u</sup>Curse not the king, no, not in thy thought; and curse not the rich in thy bed-chamber: for <sup>x</sup>a bird of the air shall carry the voice, and that which hath wings shall tell the matter.

## CHAPTER XI.

1 Directions for charity....9 The day of judgment to be thought on.

CAST thy bread <sup>a</sup>upon the waters: for thou shalt find it <sup>b</sup>after many days.

2 Give a <sup>c</sup>portion to seven, and also to eight; for thou knowest not what evil shall be upon the earth.

3 If the <sup>d</sup>clouds be full of rain, they empty *themselves* upon the earth: and if the tree fall toward the south, or toward the north, in the place where the tree falleth, there it shall be.

4 He that <sup>e</sup>observeth the wind, shall not sow; and he that regardeth the clouds, shall not reap.

5 As thou knowest not what *is* the way of the <sup>||</sup>spirit, *nor* how <sup>f</sup>the bones *do grow* in the womb of her that is with child; even so thou knowest not the works of God who maketh all.

6 In the morning sow thy <sup>g</sup>seed, and in the evening withhold not thy hand: for thou knowest not whether shall prosper, either this or that, or whether they both *shall be* alike good.

7 Truly the <sup>h</sup>light *is* sweet, and a pleasant *thing it is* for the eyes to behold the sun:

8 But if a man live many years, *and* rejoice in them all; yet let him <sup>i</sup>remember the days of darkness; for they shall be <sup>k</sup>many. All that cometh *is* vanity.

9 <sup>l</sup>Rejoice, O young man, in thy youth, and let thy heart cheer thee in the days of thy youth, and walk in the ways of thine heart, and in the sight of thine eyes: but know thou, that for all these *things* God will bring thee into <sup>m</sup>judgment.

10 Therefore remove sorrow from thy heart, and put away <sup>n</sup>evil from thy flesh: for childhood and youth *are* vanity.

## CHAPTER XII.

The fear of God is the chief antidote of vanity.

REMEMBER now thy Creator in the <sup>a</sup>days of thy youth, while the evil days come not, nor the <sup>b</sup>years draw nigh, when thou shalt say, I have no pleasure in them;

2 While the sun, or the light, or the moon, or the stars, be not darkened; nor the clouds return after the rain:

3 In the day when the <sup>c</sup>keepers of the house shall tremble, and the strong men shall bow themselves, and the grinders cease because they are few, and those that look out of the windows be darkened;

4 And the doors shall be shut in the streets, when the sound of the grinding is low; and he shall rise up at the voice of the bird; and all <sup>d</sup>the daughters of music shall be brought low:

5 Also *when* they shall be afraid of *that which is* high, and fears *shall be* in the way, and the almond-tree shall flourish, and the grasshopper shall be a burden, and desire shall fail because man goeth to his <sup>e</sup>long home, and the mourners go about the streets:

6 Or ever the silver cord be loosed, or the golden bowl be broken, or the pitcher be broken at the fountain, or the wheel broken at the cistern:

7 Then shall the <sup>f</sup>dust return to the earth as it was; and the spirit shall return <sup>g</sup>unto God who gave it.
