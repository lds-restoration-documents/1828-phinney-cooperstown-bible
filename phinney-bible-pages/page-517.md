## CHAPTER XIX.

A lamentation for the princes of Israel, and for Jerusalem.

MOREOVER, take thou <sup>a</sup>up a lamentation for the <sup>b</sup>princes of Israel,

2 And say, What *is* thy mother? <sup>c</sup>A lioness: she lay down among <sup>d</sup>lions, she nourished her <sup>e</sup>whelps among young lions.

3 And she brought up <sup>f</sup>one of her whelps: it became a young lion, and it learned to catch the prey; it devoured men.

4 The nations also heard of him; he was taken in their pit, and they brought him with chains unto the land of Egypt.

5 Now, when she saw that she had <sup>g</sup>waited, *and* her hope was lost, then she took <sup>h</sup>another of her whelps, *and* made him a young lion.

6 And he went up and down among the lions, he became a young lion, and learned to catch the prey, <sup>i</sup>*and* devoured men.

7 And he knew their <sup>k</sup>desolate palaces, and he laid waste their cities; and the land was desolate, and the fulness thereof, by the noise of his <sup>l</sup>roaring.

8 Then the <sup>m</sup>nations set against him on every side from the provinces, and spread their net over him: he was taken in their pit.

9 And they put him in ward in chains, and <sup>||</sup>brought him to the king of Babylon; they brought him into holds, that his voice should no more be heard upon the mountains of Israel.

10 Thy mother *is* like <sup>n</sup>a vine in thy blood, planted by the waters: she was fruitful, and full of branches, by reason of many waters.

11 And she had <sup>o</sup>strong rods for the sceptres of them that bare rule, and her stature was exalted among the thick branches, and she <sup>p</sup>appeared in her height with the multitude of her branches.

12 But she was <sup>q</sup>plucked up in fury, she was cast down to the ground, and the east wind dried up her fruit; her strong rods were broken and withered; <sup>r</sup>the fire consumed them.

13 And now she *is* planted in <sup>s</sup>the wilderness, in a dry and thirsty ground.

14 And <sup>t</sup>fire is gone out of a rod of her branches, *which* hath devoured her fruit, so that she hath <sup>u</sup>no strong rod *to be* a sceptre to rule. This *is* a lamentation, and shall be for a lamentation.
