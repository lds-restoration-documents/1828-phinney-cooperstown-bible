## CHAPTER XXVI.

Observations about fools, sluggards, and busy-bodies.

AS snow in summer, and as rain in harvest; so <sup>a</sup>honour is not seemly for a fool.

2 As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying; so the <sup>b</sup>curse causeless shall not come.

3 A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and <sup>c</sup>a rod for the fool's back.

4 <sup>d</sup>Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him.

5 Answer a fool <sup>e</sup>according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit.

6 He that sendeth <sup>f</sup>a message by the hand of a fool, cutteth off the feet, *and* drinketh damage.

7 The legs of the lame are not equal; so *is* a parable in the mouth of fools.

8 As he that bindeth a stone in a sling; so *is* he that <sup>g</sup>giveth honour to a fool.

9 *As* a thorn goeth up into the hand of a drunkard; so *is* a parable in the mouth of fools.

10 The great *God,* that formed all *things,* both rewardeth the fool, and rewardeth transgressors.

11 As a dog <sup>h</sup>returneth to his vomit; *so* a fool returneth to his folly.

12 Seest thou a man <sup>i</sup>wise in his own conceit? *there is* more hope of a fool than of him.

13 The slothful *man* saith, *There is* <sup>k</sup>a lion in the way, a lion *is* in the streets.

14 *As* the door turneth upon his hinges, so *doth* the slothful upon his bed.

15 The slothful hideth his hand in *his* bosom; it <sup>l</sup>grieveth him to bring it again to his mouth.

16 The sluggard *is* wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can <sup>m</sup>render a reason.

17 He that passeth by, *and* <sup>n</sup>meddleth with strife *belonging* not to him, *is like* one that taketh a dog by the ears.

18 As a mad *man,* who casteth firebrands, arrows, and death;

19 So *is* the man *that* deceiveth his neighbour, and saith, Am not I <sup>o</sup>in sport?

20 Where no <sup>p</sup>wood is, *there* the fire goeth out; so where *there is* no tale-bearer, the strife ceaseth.

21 *As* coals *are* to burning coals, and wood to fire; so *is* a <sup>q</sup>contentious man to kindle strife.

22 The words of a tale-bearer *are* as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly.

23 <sup>r</sup>Burning lips, and a wicked heart, *are like* a potsherd covered with silver dross.

24 He that hateth dissembleth with his lips, and layeth up deceit within him;

25 When he speaketh fair, <sup>s</sup>believe him not: for <!-- uncertain: word partly obscured by a blemish --> *there are* seven abominations in his heart.

26 *Whose* hatred is covered by <sup>t</sup>deceit, his wickedness shall be shewed before the *whole* congregation.

27 Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein; and he that <sup>u</sup>rolleth a stone, it will return upon him.

28 A lying tongue hateth *those that are* <sup>x</sup>afflicted by it; and a flattering mouth worketh ruin.

## CHAPTER XXVII.

Observations of self-love, and of care to avoid offences.

BOAST not thyself <sup>a</sup>of to-morrow; for thou knowest not what <sup>b</sup>a day may bring forth.

2 Let <sup>c</sup>another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips.

3 A stone *is* heavy, and the sand weighty: but <sup>d</sup>a fool's wrath *is* heavier than them both.

4 Wrath *is* cruel, and anger *is* outrageous; but who *is* able to stand before <sup>e</sup>envy?

5 <sup>f</sup>Open rebuke *is* better than <sup>g</sup>secret love.

6 Faithful *are* the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy *are* deceitful.

7 The <sup>h</sup>full soul <sup>i</sup>loatheth a honey-comb; but to the <sup>k</sup>hungry soul every bitter thing *is* <sup>l</sup>sweet.

8 As a bird that wandereth from her nest, *so is* a man that wandereth from his place.

9 Ointment and perfume rejoice the heart; so *doth* the <sup>m</sup>sweetness of a man's friend by hearty counsel.

10 <sup>n</sup>Thine own friend, and thy father's friend, forsake not; neither go into thy brother's house in the day of thy calamity: *for* better *is* a neighbour *that is* near than a brother far off.

11 My son, be wise, and make my heart glad, that I may answer him that reproacheth me.

12 A prudent *man* foreseeth the evil, *and* hideth himself: *but* the simple pass on, *and* are punished.

13 Take his garment that is surety for a stranger, and take a pledge of him for a strange woman.

14 He that blesseth his friend with <sup>o</sup>a loud voice, rising early in the morning, it shall be counted a curse to him.

15 A continual dropping in a very rainy day, and a contentious woman, are alike.

16 Whosoever hideth her hideth the wind, and the ointment of his right hand, *which* bewrayeth *itself.*

17 Iron <sup>p</sup>sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.

18 Whoso keepeth the fig-tree shall eat the fruit thereof; so he that <sup>q</sup>waiteth on his master shall be honoured.

19 As in water, face *answereth* to face; so the heart of man to man.

20 Hell and destruction are never full: <sup>r</sup>so the eyes of man are never satisfied.

21 *As* the fining-pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so *is* a man to his praise.

22 Though thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar among wheat with a pestle, *yet* will not <sup>s</sup>his foolishness depart from him.

23 Be thou diligent <sup>t</sup>to know the state of thy flocks, *and* look well to thy herds;

24 For <sup>†</sup>riches *are* not for ever: and doth the <sup>u</sup>crown *endure* to every generation?
