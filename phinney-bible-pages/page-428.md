# ¶ The SONG of SOLOMON.

## CHAPTER I.

1 The church's love unto Christ....5 She confesseth her deformity.

THE <sup>a</sup>Song of songs, which *is* Solomon's.

2 Let <sup>b</sup>him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth: for thy <sup>c</sup>love *is* better than wine.

3 Because of the <sup>d</sup>savour of thy good ointments, thy <sup>e</sup>name *is as* ointment poured forth, therefore do the virgins love thee.

4 Draw me, we will <sup>f</sup>run after thee. The King hath brought me into his <sup>g</sup>chambers: we will be glad and rejoice in thee; we will remember thy love more than wine: the upright love thee.

5 I *am* black, but <sup>h</sup>comely, O ye daughters of Jerusalem; as the tents of Kedar, as the curtains of Solomon.

6 Look not upon me, because I *am* <sup>i</sup>black, because the sun hath looked upon me: my <sup>k</sup>mother's children were angry with me; they made me the keeper of the vineyards; *but* mine own vineyard have I not kept.

7 Tell me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou <sup>l</sup>feedest, where thou makest *thy flock* to rest at noon: for why should I be as one that turneth aside by the flocks of thy companions?

8 If thou know not, O thou fairest among women, go thy way forth by the <sup>m</sup>footsteps of the flock, and feed thy kids beside the shepherds' tents.

9 I have compared thee, O <sup>n</sup>my love, to a company of horses in Pharaoh's <sup>o</sup>chariots.

10 Thy cheeks are comely with rows *of jewels,* thy neck with <sup>p</sup>chains *of gold.*

11 <sup>q</sup>We will make thee borders of gold, with studs of silver.

12 While the King *sitteth* at his <sup>r</sup>table, my spikenard sendeth forth the smell thereof.

13 A bundle of myrrh *is* my well-beloved unto me; he shall lie all night <sup>s</sup>betwixt my breasts.

14 My beloved *is* unto me *as* a cluster of <sup>t</sup>camphire in the vineyards of En-gedi.

15 Behold, thou *art* fair, my love; behold, thou *art* fair, thou <sup>u</sup>*hast* doves' eyes.

16 Behold, thou *art* fair, my beloved, yea, pleasant: also <sup>x</sup>our bed *is* green.

17 The <sup>y</sup>beams of our house *are* cedar, *and* our rafters of fir.

## CHAPTER II.

The mutual love of Christ and his church.

I *AM* the rose of Sharon, *and* the lily of the <sup>a</sup>valleys.

2 As the lily <sup>b</sup>among thorns, so *is* my love among the daughters.

3 As the apple-tree among the trees of the wood, so *is* my beloved among the sons. I sat down under his shadow with great delight, <sup>c</sup>and his fruit *was* sweet to my taste.

4 He brought me to the banqueting-house, and his banner over me *was* <sup>d</sup>love.

5 Stay me with flagons, comfort me with apples; for I *am* <sup>e</sup>sick of love.

6 His <sup>f</sup>left hand *is* under my head, and his right hand doth embrace me.

7 I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and by the hinds of the field, that ye <sup>g</sup>stir not up, nor awake *my* love, till he please.

8 The <sup>h</sup>voice of my beloved! behold, he <sup>i</sup>cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping upon the hills.

9 My beloved is like a roe, or a young hart: behold, he standeth behind <sup>k</sup>our wall, he looketh forth at the window, shewing himself through the lattice.

10 My beloved spake, and said unto me, Rise up, my love, my fair one, and <sup>l</sup>come away.

11 For, lo, the <sup>m</sup>winter is past, the rain is over *and* gone;

12 The flowers appear on the earth; the time of the <sup>n</sup>singing *of birds* is come, and the voice of the turtle is heard in our land.

13 The fig-tree putteth forth her green figs, and the vines *with* <sup>o</sup>the tender grape give a *good* smell. Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away.

14 ¶ O my dove, *that art* in the <sup>p</sup>clefts of the rock, in the secret *places* of the stairs, let me see thy countenance, let me hear thy voice; for sweet *is* thy voice, and thy countenance *is* comely.

15 Take us the <sup>q</sup>foxes, the <sup>r</sup>little foxes, that spoil the vines: for our vines *have* tender grapes.

16 ¶ My beloved *is* <sup>s</sup>mine, and I *am* his: he feedeth among the lilies.

17 Until the day <sup>†</sup>break, and the shadows flee away, turn, my beloved; and be thou like a roe, or a young hart, upon the mountains of <sup>t</sup>Bether.

## CHAPTER III.

The church's fight and victory in temptation.

BY night on <sup>a</sup>my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not.

2 I will rise now, and <sup>b</sup>go about the city; in the streets, and in the broad ways, I will seek him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not.

3 The <sup>c</sup>watchmen that go about the city found me: *to whom I said,* <sup>d</sup>Saw ye him whom my soul loveth?

4 *It was* but a little that I passed from them, but I found him whom my soul loveth: I held him, and would <sup>e</sup>not let him go, until I had brought him into my mother's house, into the chamber of her that <sup>f</sup>conceived me.

5 I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and by the hinds of the field, that ye stir not up, nor awake *my* love till he please.
