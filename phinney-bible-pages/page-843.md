8 A double-minded man *is* unstable in all his ways.

9 <sup>e</sup>Let the brother of low degree rejoice in that he is exalted;

10 <sup>f</sup>But the rich in that he is made low: because as the flower of the grass he shall pass away.

11 For the sun is no sooner risen with a burning heat, but it withereth the grass, and the flower thereof falleth, and the grace of the fashion of it perisheth: so also shall the rich man fade away in his ways.

12 <sup>g</sup>Blessed *is* the man that endureth temptation: for when he is tried, he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him.

13 <sup>h</sup>Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God: for God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man:

14 But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed.

15 <sup>i</sup>Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin; and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.

16 Do not err, my beloved brethren.

17 <sup>k</sup>Every good gift, and every perfect gift, is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.

18 <sup>l</sup>Of his own will begat he us with the word of truth, that we should be a kind of first-fruits of his creatures.

19 <sup>m</sup>Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:

20 <sup>n</sup>For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.

21 <sup>o</sup>Wherefore lay apart all filthiness and superfluity of naughtiness, and receive with meekness the engrafted word, which is able to save your souls.

22 <sup>p</sup>But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.

23 <sup>q</sup>For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass:

24 For he beholdeth himself and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was.

25 <sup>r</sup>But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth *therein,* he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.

26 <sup>s</sup>If any man among you seem to be religious, and bridleth not his tongue, but deceiveth his own heart, this man's religion *is* vain.

27 <sup>t</sup>Pure religion, and undefiled before God and the Father, is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, *and* to keep himself unspotted from the world.

## CHAPTER II.

1 We must not regard the rich, and despise the poor....10 The guilt of any one breach of the law....17 Faith without works is dead.

<sup>a</sup>MY brethren, have not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, *the Lord* of glory, with respect of persons.

2 For if there come unto your assemb'ly a man with a gold ring, in goodly apparel; and there come in also a poor man in vile raiment; <!-- uncertain: printed "assemb'ly" with an apostrophe-like mark -->

3 <sup>b</sup>And ye have respect to him that weareth the gay clothing, and say unto him, Sit thou here in a good place; and say to the poor, Stand thou there, or sit here under my footstool:

4 <sup>c</sup>Are ye not then partial in yourselves, and are become judges of evil thoughts?

5 <sup>d</sup>Hearken, my beloved brethren, Hath not God chosen the poor of this world, rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which he hath promised to them that love him?

6 <sup>e</sup>But ye have despised the poor. Do not rich men oppress you, and draw you before the judgment-seats?

7 Do not they blaspheme that worthy name by the which ye are called?

8 <sup>f</sup>If ye fulfil the royal law, according to the scripture, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, ye do well:

9 <sup>g</sup>But if ye have respect to persons, ye commit sin, and are convinced of the law as transgressors.

10 <sup>h</sup>For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one *point,* he is guilty of all.

11 <sup>i</sup>For he that said, Do not commit adultery, said also, Do not kill. Now if thou commit no adultery, yet if thou kill, thou art become a transgressor of the law.

12 <sup>k</sup>So speak ye, and so do, as they that shall be judged by the law of liberty.

13 <sup>l</sup>For he shall have judgment without mercy, that hath shewed no mercy, and mercy rejoiceth against judgment.

14 <sup>m</sup>What *doth it* profit, my brethren, though a man say he hath faith, and have not works? Can faith save him?

15 <sup>n</sup>If a brother or sister be naked, and destitute of daily food,

16 And one of you say unto them, Depart in peace, be *ye* warmed and filled; notwithstanding ye give them not those things which are needful to the body; what *doth it* profit?

17 Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone.

18 Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works.

19 <sup>o</sup>Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.

20 But wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead?

21 <sup>p</sup>Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar?

22 <sup>q</sup>Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect?

23 <sup>r</sup>And the scripture was fulfilled, which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.

24 Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only.

25 <sup>s</sup>Likewise also was not Rahab the harlot justified by works, when she had received the messengers, and had sent *them* out another way?

26 For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.
