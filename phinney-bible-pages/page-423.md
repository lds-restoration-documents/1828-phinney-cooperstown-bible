# ¶ ECCLESIASTES, or the PREACHER.

## CHAPTER I.

The Preacher sheweth that all human courses are vain.

THE words of the Preacher, the son of David, <sup>a</sup>king of Jerusalem.

2 Vanity of vanities, saith the Preacher, vanity of vanities; <sup>b</sup>all *is* vanity.

3 What profit hath a man of all his labour which he <sup>c</sup>taketh under the sun?

4 *One* generation passeth away, and *another* generation cometh: but the <sup>d</sup>earth abideth for ever.

5 The sun also ariseth, and the sun goeth down, and <sup>e</sup>hasteth to his place where he arose.

6 The wind goeth toward the south, and turneth about unto the north: it <sup>f</sup>whirleth about continually; and the wind returneth again according to his circuits.

7 All the rivers run into the sea; yet the sea *is* not full: unto the place from whence the rivers come, thither they <sup>g</sup>return again.

8 All things *are* full of labour, man cannot utter *it:* the <sup>h</sup>eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.

9 The thing that hath been, it *is that* which shall be; and that which is done, *is* that which shall be done: and *there is* no <sup>i</sup>new *thing* under the sun.

10 Is there *any* thing whereof it may be said, See, <sup>k</sup>this is new? it hath been already of old time, which was before us.

11 *There is* <sup>l</sup>no remembrance of former *things;* neither shall there be *any* remembrance of *things* that are to come with *those* that shall come after.

12 ¶ <sup>m</sup>I the Preacher was king over Israel in Jerusalem:

13 And I <sup>n</sup>gave my heart to seek and search out by wisdom concerning <sup>o</sup>all *things* that are done under heaven: this sore travail hath God given to the sons of man, to be exercised therewith.

14 I have <sup>p</sup>seen all the works that are done under the sun; and, behold, all *is* vanity and vexation of spirit.

15 *That which is* <sup>q</sup>crooked cannot be made straight; and that which is wanting cannot be numbered.

16 I communed with mine own heart, saying, Lo, I am come to great estate, and have gotten more wisdom than all *they* that have been before me in Jerusalem; yea, my heart had <sup>r</sup>great experience of wisdom and knowledge.

17 And I <sup>s</sup>gave my heart to know wisdom, and to know <sup>t</sup>madness and folly: I perceived that this also is vexation of spirit.

18 For in much wisdom *is* much <sup>u</sup>grief; and he that increaseth knowledge increaseth sorrow.

## CHAPTER II.

The vanity of human courses in the works of pleasure

I SAID <sup>a</sup>in mine heart, Go to now, I will prove thee with mirth; therefore enjoy pleasure; and, behold, this also *is* <sup>b</sup>vanity.

2 I said <sup>c</sup>of laughter, *It is* mad: and of mirth, What doeth it?

3 I sought in mine heart to give myself unto wine, (<sup>d</sup>yet acquainting mine heart with wisdom,) and to lay hold on folly, till I might see what *was* that good for the sons of men which they should do under the heaven all the days of their life.

4 I <sup>e</sup>made me great works; I builded me <sup>f</sup>houses; I planted me vineyards;

5 I made me gardens and <sup>g</sup>orchards, and I planted trees in them <sup>h</sup>of all *kind of* fruits;

6 I made me <sup>i</sup>pools of water, to water therewith the wood that bringeth forth trees;

7 I got *me* servants and maidens, and had servants born in my house; also I had <sup>k</sup>great possessions of great and small cattle above all that were in Jerusalem before me;

8 I gathered me also silver and gold, and the peculiar treasure of kings and of the provinces; I gat me men-singers and women-singers, and the delights of the sons of men, *as* <sup>l</sup>musical instruments, and that of all sorts.

9 So I was great, and increased more than all that were before me in Jerusalem: also my wisdom <sup>m</sup>remained with me.

10 And whatsoever <sup>n</sup>mine eyes desired I kept not from them: I withheld not my heart from any joy: for <sup>o</sup>my heart rejoiced in all my labour: and this was my portion of all my labour.

11 Then I <sup>p</sup>looked on all the works that my hands had wrought, and on the labour that I had laboured to do: and, behold, all *was* vanity and vexation of spirit, and *there was* <sup>q</sup>no profit under the sun.

12 And I turned myself to behold wisdom, and <sup>r</sup>madness, and folly: for what *can* the man *do* that cometh <sup>s</sup>after the king? *even* that which hath been already done.

13 Then I saw that <sup>t</sup>wisdom excelleth folly, as far as light excelleth darkness.

14 The wise man's eyes *are* in his head, but the fool walketh in darkness: and I myself perceived also that <sup>u</sup>one event happeneth to them all.

15 Then said I in my heart, As it happeneth to the fool, so it happeneth even to me: and why was I then <sup>x</sup>more wise? Then I said in my heart, that this also *is* vanity.

16 For *there is* no remembrance of the wise more than of the fool for ever; seeing that which now *is,* in the days to come shall be <sup>y</sup>forgotten. And how dieth the wise *man?* <sup>z</sup>As the fool.

17 Therefore I <sup>a</sup>hated life; because the work that is wrought under the sun *is* grievous unto me: for all *is* vanity and vexation of spirit.

18 Yea, <sup>b</sup>I hated all my labour which I had taken under the sun; because I should leave it unto the man that shall be after me.

19 And who knoweth whether he shall be a wise *man* <sup>c</sup>or a fool? yet shall he have rule over all my labour wherein I have laboured, and wherein I have shewed myself wise under the sun. This *is* also <sup>d</sup>vanity.

20 Therefore I went about to cause my heart <sup>e</sup>to despair of all the labour which I took under the sun.

21 For there is a man whose labour *is* in wisdom, and in knowledge, and in equity; yet to a man that hath not laboured therein shall he <sup>f</sup>leave it *for* his portion. This also *is* vanity, and a great evil.

22 For what hath man of <sup>g</sup>all his labour, and of the vexation of his heart, wherein he hath laboured under the sun?

23 For <sup>h</sup>all his days *are* sorrows, and his travail grief; yea, his heart taketh not <sup>i</sup>rest in the night. This is also vanity.
